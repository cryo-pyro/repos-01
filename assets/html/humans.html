Section II

Three Blind
Men and an Algorithm

1

Tom Phillips

Humans: A Brief History of How We F*cked It All Up

History as comedy of errors — cognitive failures, moral lapses, spectacular stupidity catalogued with wit.

What the algorithm sees

The errors are ε — the stochastic noise term without which no system can escape a local minimum. Every catastrophe is the species' distributed R&D running a necessary search function in the dark. The Austrian army attacking itself is the gradient probe. The f*ck up was never a glitch.

2

Yuval Noah Harari

Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind

The Cognitive Revolution gave us shared fictions — money, law, religion — that let millions of strangers cooperate.

What the algorithm sees

The fictions are UI/UX. Once a high-variance scout finds a better basin, the discovery must be compressed into a transmissible format so the colony can descend without bearing the exploration cost. Harari's fictions are pheromone highways paved over the gradient. They are the Apollonian smoothing of a Dionysian discovery.

3

Robert Sapolsky

Behave + Determined

Every behavior is the downstream output of a causal chain stretching from milliseconds to millennia. There is no free will — only a very long calculation.

What the algorithm sees

Sapolsky is the only one who refused compression. His retroductive method — tracing backwards from behavior through hormones through epigenetics through evolution — is structurally identical to the stratigraphic method. He calls the layers "one second before" instead of Body → Calibrate. Same algorithm, different vocabulary.

"Compression destroys the gradient."
