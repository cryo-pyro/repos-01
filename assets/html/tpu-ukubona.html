0 scalar a single number ▶

A scalar is just one number. Temperature. Price. Age. The number 7. No direction. No structure. Just a magnitude.

In your system, the most important scalar is the loss — the total UGX leakage this week. One number that tells you how wrong things are. Everything in the hierarchy exists to produce this number honestly.

Einstein called it a rank-0 tensor. It's a tensor with zero dimensions. A degenerate case. The bottom of the hierarchy.

1 tensor the full landscape ▶

A tensor is not just a "big array." That's what confused you — and it's what most explanations get wrong. An array is a storage format. A tensor is a relationship that holds across any coordinate system.

Einstein needed tensors because the laws of physics had to be true from any observer's frame — whether you're standing still, moving fast, or in a gravitational field. The tensor guaranteed that.

Google needed tensors (TPU = Tensor Processing Unit) because a neural network's weights are relationships across millions of dimensions simultaneously — and you need to update all of them at once.

You need a tensor because your project exists in three dimensions simultaneously, and a spreadsheet can only show you one slice at a time.

Your Rank-3 Tensor

T[Procurement, Pre_Con, Budget] = ...

93 variables. All held at once.

The cube in the diagram is not decoration. It is literally showing you three axes intersecting. Each cell in that cube is one of Ibo's 93 variables.
2 vector one snapshot of reality ▶

A vector is a list of numbers — but the numbers are coordinates in a space, not just a random list. The difference matters: a shopping list is an array. A vector knows where it lives.

In physics: position + velocity. Two numbers. That's all you need to know a particle's future. In your system: one project snapshot. One row of reality.

Project state vector — this week

v = [budget_remaining, cement_on_site,

workers_active, tasks_done, leakage]

v = [42,000,000, 80, 14, 37, 1,650,000]

A vector is a slice of the tensor — one point in the landscape, at one moment in time. The tensor contains all possible vectors.

3 matrix transformation and process ▶

A matrix takes a vector and produces another vector. It encodes a rule for how things change. Not what things are — how they move.

In classical mechanics: multiply your state vector (position, velocity) by a matrix to get next week's state. The matrix IS the physics.

In your system: your procurement policy is a matrix. Your construction workflow is a matrix. The financial flows between departments are a matrix. They act on vectors (project states) to produce the next state.

The key equation

state_next = M × state_current

Ibo's operations manual IS the matrix M.

Encoded in 93 variables.

Now computable.

The leakage matrix we built — weeks as rows, material categories as columns, UGX leakage as values — is exactly this. It shows how the project's state evolves over time.

4 eigen what the system wants to do ▶

"Eigen" is German for inherent or characteristic. An eigenvector of a matrix is a direction that the matrix doesn't rotate — it only stretches or shrinks it. These are the system's natural modes.

Most vectors get rotated by a matrix. But eigenvectors survive the transformation pointing in the same direction. They reveal the structure underneath the noise.

What eigen tells you

Leakage matrix → eigen decomposition

Mode 1 eigenvalue: 56,000,000 (dominant)

Mode 2 eigenvalue: 150,000

Eigenvector 1: Cement=0.981, Labor=0.06

"This system has one dominant failure mode.

It runs through cement. Every week. Always."

This is the difference between observation and diagnosis. A spreadsheet tells you that cement numbers look bad this week. Eigen tells you that cement has been the structural failure mode for 12 weeks — and that it's not random, it's systemic.

Eigen is NOT a data structure. It is a property of a matrix — what you discover inside it. That's why it sits outside the tensor/vector/matrix stack in the diagram.
5 sgd optimization — closing the loop ▶

Stochastic Gradient Descent. The engine that trains every neural network that has ever existed. It does one thing: move toward lower loss.

You have a scalar loss (UGX leakage). You have a landscape (the tensor). SGD finds the direction that reduces loss fastest and takes a step in that direction. Repeat until the loss is near zero.

In ML: the system adjusts millions of weights automatically. In your system: SGD is you reading the eigen diagnosis and changing the process. You adjust the procurement contract. You add a gate check at cement delivery. You run it again. Loss drops.

One SGD cycle — Strata Stone

1. Compute loss: UGX 1,650,000

2. Run eigen: cement is dominant (0.981)

3. Gradient: cement gate gap = main driver

4. Action: require photo + signature at delivery

5. Next week: recompute loss

6. Loss drops → system is learning

This is why the Pentad matters. Without the full loop — landscape → state → process → diagnosis → action — you have dashboards. With the loop, you have a system that gets better over time. Automatically.

Quick reference

scalar

One number. Temperature, price, loss. Rank-0 tensor. The verdict of the whole system.

vector

A list of numbers that knows its coordinates. One snapshot of reality. A point in the landscape.

matrix

A grid of numbers that transforms vectors. Encodes rules, processes, flows. Your operations manual as math.

tensor

Relationships that hold across any coordinate system. Not just an array — a structure that survives transformation. Google's TPU runs on this.

eigen

The natural modes of a matrix. Directions that survive transformation. Separates systemic failure from random noise.

sgd

Stochastic Gradient Descent. The engine that moves toward lower loss. Trains every neural network. Closes the loop.
