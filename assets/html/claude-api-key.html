What it is

Not a chatbot. A model that learns.

Most health apps give you information. This twin does something different — it holds a live model of your specific condition and updates that model as new evidence arrives. Think of it less like a search engine and more like a clinician who is always listening, never forgetting, and getting sharper over time.

The navigation analogy

A ship's GPS doesn't just know where you are once. It continuously takes in signals — speed, heading, depth — and updates its estimate of position. The longer it runs, the more accurate it becomes. Your learning twin works the same way, but for your health.

Five lenses. One loop.

How it works

What actually happens when you push an update

1
perceiver

You share something new

Type a note, speak into your phone, upload a photo of your skin, or drop in a PDF from your doctor. The twin accepts anything — it is fully multimodal.

2
gradient computer

An AI reads the signal

A large language model — acting as a clinical reasoning engine — analyses what you shared and returns a gradient vector: which diagnoses this evidence supports or argues against, and how strongly.

3
update rule

The belief state shifts

The twin applies the gradient to its current probability distribution across diagnoses. The shift is scaled by a learning rate — bigger for hard evidence like lab results, smaller for subjective descriptions.

4
fractal property

The new state becomes the foundation

θₜ₊₁ becomes the new θₜ. The updated twin state is saved as a versioned snapshot. Every past version is preserved. Ukubona is not the end — the loop continues with each new piece of information.

Questions

What people ask

Is this a replacement for my doctor?
No — and it is not designed to be. The twin is a reasoning tool that helps you track, articulate, and understand your condition better. It does not prescribe, diagnose definitively, or replace clinical judgement. Think of it as a sophisticated health journal that can reason about what you share.
Where does my data go?
Your observations are sent directly from your browser to the Anthropic API for analysis, and then discarded. The twin state is stored only in your browser's local memory and in the versioned JSON files you choose to download. There is no cloud storage, no account, no database. What you download is yours.
What does "belief state" mean?
The twin maintains a probability distribution across a set of candidate diagnoses — for example, aquagenic pruritus 68%, xerosis 17%, urticaria 6%, polycythemia vera 9%. This is the "belief state." Every new observation shifts these probabilities according to how much the evidence supports or argues against each diagnosis. It is not a final answer; it is a live estimate that gets sharper as more evidence arrives.
What is η (eta)?
η is the learning rate — a number between 0 and 1 that controls how much a single piece of evidence can shift the belief state. A low η (like 0.2 for text) means the twin updates cautiously; one description cannot overturn accumulated evidence. A high η (like 0.8 for a lab result) means strong clinical data creates a more decisive shift. You can override the default for any update.
What does "θₜ₊₁ becomes new θₜ" mean?
This is the fractal property of the Ukubona framework. After each update, the new state becomes the starting point for the next cycle. The twin never resets to zero — it carries the accumulated evidence of every previous update forward. This is what makes it a learning system rather than a static record.
Can I use this for conditions other than skin?
Yes. The Ukubona Pentad framework is general. The current twin is configured for a dermatology differential, but the same architecture can be adapted to any condition with a defined diagnostic space. The framework has been applied conceptually to cardiology, neurology, and chronic disease management.
What do I do with the downloaded JSON files?
Three options. First, keep them as your personal health record — a timestamped log of every observation and how the twin's understanding shifted. Second, share them with your clinician as a structured summary of your condition over time. Third, drop them back into a future version of the twin to restore a previous state — the fractal loop continues.
