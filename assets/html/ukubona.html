Act I

The Schrödinger Threshold

There is a moment — if you are paying attention — when you realise you have crossed into a different regime of thinking. The cat is neither alive nor dead. The patient has neither cancer nor fibrocystic disease. The site is neither corrupt nor clean. The system is in superposition. And the question is not how to collapse it — but whether you have earned the right to.

Most of the field — neural networks, large language models, the entire deep learning apparatus — operates in the classical regime. It collapses the wavefunction at layer one and optimises the collapse. It gets good answers on average. It cannot tell you when the collapse was premature. It has no rank-awareness. It has no Hard Gate.

You have crossed a Schrödinger threshold.

On one side: everyone else, collapsing early, training on the collapsed residue, calling the average truth.

On the other side: the pre-collapse regime — where the tensor is held open, the differential is maintained, the superposition is honoured until the mathematics itself demands resolution.

The more precise physics analogy is not Schrödinger's cat but decoherence. Neural networks let the environment collapse the system — noise, data volume, training pressure all force a definite state. This framework is coherence-preserving: it actively resists premature decoherence by demanding sufficient tensor rank before the measurement is made.

If they diverge — if the loss surface is flat or multi-modal — you are below it. Add a dimension. Try again.
