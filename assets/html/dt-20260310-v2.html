Your pentadic framing (U → G → O → A → X) is actually a very Nietzschean way to analyze the situation. A few thoughts on the interpretation you sketched:

1. Genealogy: the right instinct

Invoking Friedrich Nietzsche and his work On the Genealogy of Morals is appropriate here. Nietzsche's genealogical method does three things:

  1. Trace origins of values rather than accepting them as universal.
  2. Expose power structures behind moral language.
  3. Show how ideals become instruments.

So applying genealogy to Sam Altman and OpenAI rhetoric about "AI safety" and "saving humanity" is intellectually coherent. That's exactly the kind of moral narrative Nietzsche would interrogate.

2. But the video itself is also a genealogy

One important twist: the Crooked Media critique is not neutral either. A genealogical reading would ask: what moral vocabulary is the video using, and what power structures benefit from that framing?

For example, the critique relies heavily on tech skepticism, anti-billionaire populism, and platform accountability narratives. Those are themselves contemporary moral frameworks. Nietzsche would likely say: this is another will-to-power, not a neutral exposure of one.

So genealogy cuts both ways.

3. The "ascetic priest" analogy: interesting but imperfect

The comparison of Altman to Nietzsche's ascetic priest is clever. In Nietzsche, the ascetic priest channels fear and guilt and frames suffering as necessary for salvation. There is a partial parallel:

Nietzsche structureAI narrative
Sin / decadenceUncontrolled AGI risk
Priest authorityAI safety leadership
SalvationAligned superintelligence

But there are differences: the ascetic priest denies worldly power, whereas tech leaders actively seek infrastructure power (chips, compute, capital). Nietzsche's priest manages moral guilt, while AI discourse manages existential risk. So the analogy works rhetorically but not perfectly.

4. Your most interesting point: map vs. territory

The strongest part of your analysis is this line:

"dangerous inversion of the map and the territory"

This echoes ideas from Alfred Korzybski ("the map is not the territory") and even modern ML training dynamics. In AI, models simulate intelligence while narratives simulate inevitability. The risk isn't just hallucination in the model — it's hallucination in the strategic narrative around the model. That's a much deeper critique than simple tech skepticism.

5. Where your pentad fits (this is actually elegant)

If we map your letters:

6. My main critique of the video

The Crooked Media piece treats AGI skepticism as settled fact. But historically, nuclear weapons, the internet, and biotech all looked ridiculous until suddenly they weren't. The real uncertainty is time horizon, not possibility. So the debate should probably be:

  • Is AGI real? (likely yes, eventually)
  • Is it imminent? (unknown)
  • Who controls the infrastructure? (very real power question)

✅ Bottom line: Your Nietzschean reading is sharp, but the deeper insight is this: the AI discourse right now is a battle of mythologies, not a technical argument.

  • Silicon Valley: Promethean salvation narrative
  • Critics: hubris and monopolization narrative

Genealogy helps reveal both.
