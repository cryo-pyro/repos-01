Autism Spectrum Disorder · Uganda

The invisible diagnosis.

ASD prevalence in Uganda is estimated to be comparable to global rates — but diagnosis, recognition, and support remain critically limited. The gap between screening prevalence (4%) and confirmed diagnosis (1%) tells the story.

The 3:1 Ratio · Gender Gap in ASD Diagnosis

Boys are seen.
Girls are invisible.

The 3:1 male-to-female diagnosis ratio in Uganda (consistent with global data) is not evidence that boys have autism more often — it is evidence that existing screening tools and clinical frameworks are calibrated to male presentation. Girls with ASD are systematically missed.

Workforce · The Human Resource Crisis

53 psychiatrists.
50 million people.

The structural foundation for addressing autism and mental health in Uganda is critically under-resourced. What follows is the real arithmetic of the treatment gap.

Psychiatrists · Uganda total
53
For ~50 million people. Most are based in Kampala. Only one was historically based outside the capital — at Mbarara University (MUST).
Lancet Psychiatry 2022 · earlier WHO-AIMS: 0.08/100K
Psychiatric beds near Kampala
62%
62% of all psychiatric beds are in or near Kampala. 87% of Uganda's population lives in rural areas. The math does not work.
WHO-AIMS Uganda · GlobeMed Northwestern 2020
Psychologists per 100K
0.01
One psychologist per 10 million people. Social workers: 0.01/100K. Occupational therapists: 0.01/100K. The autism therapy workforce is essentially non-existent outside Kampala.
WHO-AIMS Uganda
Mental health budget (% healthcare)
1%
Less than 1% of the healthcare budget. The vast majority goes to Butabika Hospital for severe psychiatric inpatient care. Community, neurodevelopmental, and child mental health receive almost nothing.
Sub-Saharan Africa MH Review, PMC 2025
Inpatient psychiatric units (all Uganda)
28
28 inpatient units across Uganda. One national mental hospital (Butabika, 500 beds, frequently overcrowded). No separate children's psychiatric facility.
GlobeMed Northwestern 2020 · WHO-AIMS
Mental health cases, surge 2020–2025
+71%
NPA Feb 2026 data: facility-reported cases surged from 494,326 to 843,295. NPA has classified mental health as Uganda's #1 planning priority. The silent emergency.

The Amaani Response · What the Data Demands

Six gaps.
One loop.

The data above defines the problem. Amaani's closed-loop system is designed to address each gap — not separately, but as an integrated pentadic system.

Diagnosis Gap · L1
~38,800 estimated ASD cases. Less than 1% formally diagnosed.
The gap between screening prevalence (4%) and confirmed diagnosis (1%) across Africa represents tens of thousands of children who are invisible to the health system.
→ Amaani response: MUST app deployment for community-level screening in schools
Gender Gap · L1
Girls with ASD are systematically missed by current tools.
The 3:1 ratio is a measurement artifact, not a biological reality. Screening instruments calibrated to male ASD presentation produce false negatives for girls in Uganda and across Africa.
→ Amaani response: Gender-stratified data collection from Field, feeding model refinement in Engine
Caregiver Gap · L2
Families carry the full burden without structured support.
With almost no community-based support structures, families — primarily mothers — absorb the full physical, financial, and psychological cost of ASD care in informal kin-networks.
→ Amaani response: Kin-network support programs, caregiver training, household-level data (L2 twin)
Workforce Gap · L3
0.01 psychologists per 100,000 people.
The clinical workforce for autism support is effectively absent outside Kampala. Therapy, behavioral intervention, and specialist support are inaccessible to 87% of the population.
→ Amaani response: MUST student capacity building, task-shifted tech deployment, school-based intervention
Policy Gap · L4
Mental health policies exist. Implementation does not.
Uganda has had a Mental Health Act since 1964. The 2000 mental health policy was still in draft form in 2006. The gap between policy intent and funded implementation defines the regulatory layer.
→ Amaani response: Ministry of Health + MoE data partnerships, district-level loss mapping (L4 twin)
Data Gap · L5
No national autism prevalence dataset exists.
The NPA has declared mental health its #1 priority but lacks the granular, district-level data to plan effectively. There is no longitudinal autism data at national scale.
→ Amaani response: 133-district data pipeline to NPA, long-term health cartography (L5 civilizational twin)
