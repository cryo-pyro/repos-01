The Scale of the Crisis

How Big Is Uganda's Kidney Crisis?

Kidney disease is largely silent until more than half of kidney function is already lost. By the time patients reach a clinic, options are few, expensive, and geographically concentrated in Kampala. These numbers give the scale. The case in the banner above gives the face.

2–7%
General population with CKD
Between 2 and 7 in every hundred Ugandans have some form of chronic kidney disease. Among people living with HIV — roughly 1.4 million Ugandans — the rate rises to 15 in every hundred. Most have no idea.
PMC9438417 · Global Dialysis Perspective: Uganda
12.5%
CKD prevalence in eastern Uganda
Eastern Uganda carries more than three times the kidney disease burden of southwestern Uganda (3.9%). A difference this large within a single country demands a district-level investigation — and a 146-district atlas to understand why.
Muiru et al. · SEARCH-CKD · PLOS One 2020
134+
Districts with no published kidney data
Of Uganda's 146 administrative districts (2026 Electoral Commission), fewer than twelve have any published kidney disease data. The remaining 134 — home to tens of millions — are a complete blank on the scientific map. This is the gap we are closing.
Ukubona Atlas Survey · 2026 · Updated to 146 districts per Basaza correction
4
Dialysis centres in the whole country
Uganda's 46 million people are served by four dialysis centres, concentrated in Kampala and regional towns. A patient in Kaabong or Kotido faces a journey of hundreds of kilometres for each treatment.
Daily Monitor Uganda · 2024
≈ 0
Domestic kidney transplants active
Uganda's first domestic transplants were performed at Mulago in 2023 — fewer than ten before the programme was suspended pending a regulatory framework. Every patient who needs a transplant is referred abroad. The case in this brief's banner is not an outlier. It is the rule.
Mulago pilot suspended 2024 · Monitor Uganda
$30–55K
Cost of transplant abroad per patient
A kidney transplant in India — the most common destination — costs $25,000–$35,000 out of pocket. Emergency cases reach $55,000. A domestic programme could deliver the same procedure for ~$8,000. This brief was being drafted when a classmate's $55,000 appeal arrived.
Africanews · Parliament debate 2022 · Live case, March 2026

"In Uganda, about two percent of the population have end-stage kidney disease, and many are aware that non-communicable diseases are on the rise — twenty-five percent of the population are hypertensive."

— Uganda Ministry of Health · Mulago programme briefing · 2024

The transplant pathway today
Step 1
Patient presents with end-stage renal disease at Mulago or Kiruddu. Dialysis begins — if a slot is available.
Step 2
Family launches fundraising appeal. Target: $30,000–$55,000 USD. Most campaigns fail to reach target.
Step 3
If funds raised: travel to India, Turkey, or Kenya. 3–6 month stay for surgery + recovery. Family accompanies.
Step 4
Returns to Uganda. No domestic immunosuppression monitoring infrastructure. Long-term outcomes poorly tracked.

What We Build Together

Six Deliverables.
Three Years.

01
146-District CKD Prevalence Atlas
The first comprehensive, district-level mapping of kidney disease burden across all 146 Ugandan administrative districts. Real WGS84 coordinates. ROYGBIV loss gradient. Swap-ready for MoH DHIS2 data.
Year 1 · Phase I
02
Real-Time Case Registry
A structured intake system for end-stage renal cases — capturing district of origin, aetiology, cost pathways, and referral destination. Turns individual crises into longitudinal epidemiological data.
Year 1 · Ongoing
03
Risk Factor Stratification Model
District-level modelling of hypertension, HIV, diabetes, and unknown-aetiology CKD. Bayesian update from synthetic Phase I priors to real UBOS and clinic data in Phase II.
Year 2 · Phase II
04
Transplant Cost-Benefit Framework
Economic modelling of domestic vs. international transplant pathways. $8,000 domestic vs. $30,000–$55,000 abroad. The argument for Mulago programme reinstatement — in a form NIH and Wellcome will fund.
Year 2 · Policy
05
UCU Nephrology Research Curriculum
Co-developed with Uganda Christian University: a research training programme that embeds district-level data collection into UCU's medical education. Creates the next generation of Ugandan renal researchers.
Year 2–3 · Institutional
06
NPA Policy Brief + 50-Year Cartography
Civilizational-scale (L5) modelling for the National Planning Authority. Integrates CKD burden with hypertension trends, urbanisation, and NCD projections. The 50-year health cartography that the NPA's strategic plan requires.

The Atlas sits at the intersection of four major funding priorities. The vignette above — a classmate's $55,000 transplant appeal — is the narrative anchor that makes each of these proposals concrete.

Joint Attribution

02
Data Sovereignty
All Uganda-derived data is jointly owned by Ukubona LLC and UCU. No external party may access raw data without written consent of both PIs. Processed/anonymised outputs are open access.

Capacity Transfer
