Ukubona Clinical Thesis

The Claim in Full

Medicine has a structural error. It is not missing data, inadequate training, insufficient regulation, or even negligence in the ordinary sense. It is this: the body on the table is being updated — irreversibly — by an optimizer whose loss function was never verified against the patient's own.

Thesis
Consent is not agreement. Consent is verified alignment between a patient's values and the trajectory imposed on their body. Everything else is paperwork.
Every patient arrives as a parameter space (θt) shaped by a calibration history — age, language, culture, prior surgeries, the word "conservative" meaning what it means in their world, not yours. That calibration encodes a loss function they alone can specify. The gradient is their moral direction. The scalpel is the step. The body that wakes up is θ′. No irreversible update without verified Ltotal alignment.

Six independent research trajectories — empirical, quantitative, epidemiological, philosophical, structural, and computational — have been converging on this claim from different directions. They did not plan to meet. They met anyway.

What they are trying to minimize: dignity, mobility, independence, family burden, fear of irreversible decline. This function is theirs alone to specify. Medicine almost never asks for it.

The direction of steepest improvement in the patient's actual landscape. Currently computed by the surgeon using an institutional loss function the patient never saw. This is the misalignment.

The surgical step. Taken at a learning rate that admits no correction. Driven by an alien gradient. There is no rollback. No retraining. No second pass. This is why the loss function must be verified before, not after.

The Aftermath
The body that wakes up — or doesn't. The paralysis no one named. The son in the hallway. The gun. ε is not error in the statistical sense. It is the accumulated divergence between the trajectory the optimizer chose and the one the patient needed.
Node 01 · Empirical · Makerere University

Kituuka: The Empirical Ground

Dr. Olivia Kituuka is a GI and Hepato-Pancreato-Biliary surgeon who has worked at Mulago Hospital, Kampala, for over 15 years. She recently completed a PhD in Bioethics at Makerere, supported by the Johns Hopkins Bioethics Institute, on the informed consent process for emergency surgery in urban Uganda.

Her methods were comprehensive and granular: a cross-sectional survey of patients and next of kin, in-depth interviews, key informant interviews of emergency staff, and direct observation at one public and one private hospital in Kampala. What she found was systematic.

Kituuka's Core Finding

Inadequate disclosure of risks and alternative options, combined with poor patient understanding — not due to stupidity or negligence, but due to a structural failure of the consent process itself. The clinician is unilaterally hijacking the optimization engine. The patient is reduced from a sovereign node to a passive, unweighted constraint.

The mapping onto the Ukubona framework is exact. When risks and alternatives are omitted, the patient is robbed of agency to assign their personalized weights wi. The surgeon silently substitutes an institutional loss function — optimizing for mechanical survival, throughput, and liability — in place of the patient's uncompressed dimensions. The gradient is then computed on the wrong objective. The scalpel moves in the wrong direction.

§2.1 Why Layer 4 Can't Fix a Layer 1 Problem

Kituuka's recommendations — national policies, guidelines, education programs — are Layer 4 interventions (Government/Regulatory) attempting to correct Layer 1 failures (Clinical). The structural problem is that policies are static documents written in ministry offices. Surgery is high-velocity gradient descent happening at 2am in an under-resourced trauma bay.

The Structural Gap
"A guideline written in a ministry office cannot account for the ε of a specific midnight emergency."

Layer 4 regulation creates friction in the system (L₄) without solving the clinical loss (L₁). What is needed is not a better form but an antifragile consent architecture — modular, self-rooting, functional in noise. The cassava stick, not the ministry memo.

This is the exact problem Ukubona's clinical interface is designed to address: a high-bandwidth tool that translates high-dimensional risk into the patient's vocabulary in real time, operable in the chaos of the trauma bay, not requiring a quiet office.

Nodes 02, 03, 04 · Quantitative · Johns Hopkins

The K08 Papers: Risk Made Visible

The K08-AG065520 award (Abimereki D. Muzaale, Johns Hopkins) funded the development of perioperative and long-term risk models for older live kidney donors. The two JAMA papers are the empirical foundation of that work.

PMID 20215610 · JAMA 2010
Perioperative Mortality and Long-term Survival Following Live Kidney Donation
Segev DL, Muzaale AD, et al. · JAMA 2010;303(10):959–966 · doi:10.1001/jama.2010.237
80,347 live donors · Surgical mortality: 3.1/10,000 overall · 7.6/10,000 Black donors · Long-term survival comparable to matched NHANES III cohort
PMID 24519297 · JAMA 2014
Risk of End-Stage Renal Disease Following Live Kidney Donation
Muzaale AD, Massie AB, et al. · JAMA 2014;311(6):579–586 · doi:10.1001/jama.2013.285141
96,217 donors · 15-yr ESRD risk: 30.8/10,000 donors vs 3.9/10,000 healthy nondonors · Lifetime risk: 90/10,000 donors vs 14/10,000 healthy controls

§3.1 Why Probabilities Alone Are Not Consent

These papers quantify the stakes with extraordinary precision. They are moral infrastructure in embryo. But they contain a structural incompleteness that the consent framework exposes directly.

Patient 15-yr ESRD risk w(independence) w(donor altruism) Decision
Patient A 30.8 / 10,000 0.20 0.80 Donate
Patient B 30.8 / 10,000 0.80 0.20 Decline

Same probabilities. Different wi. Opposite decisions. The papers produce the number; the consent process must produce the weights. Without weight elicitation, even the most precise risk calculator just produces better-informed misalignment.

The K08 Implication

The K08 risk calculator for ESRD, mortality, and sentinel hospitalization is moral infrastructure only when it pairs probability with preference elicitation. Each datapoint is not just an outcome — it is a life lived under a specific weight configuration. The failure of most risk calculators is that they collapse that into probability and discard the weights that made those outcomes meaningful.

§3.2 The Racial Disparity Signal

The 2010 paper reports surgical mortality of 7.6 per 10,000 for Black donors versus 2.6 for white donors — a threefold difference. The 2014 paper shows lifetime ESRD risk of 326 per 10,000 for the general (unscreened) population versus 90 per 10,000 for donors. These are not technical footnotes. They are direct inputs to a consent conversation. A Black patient in their 60s deciding whether to donate a kidney deserves a consent process that surfaces exactly this disparity, in their vocabulary, before the gradient step is taken.

Node 05 · Philosophical · Johns Hopkins · September 2010

The Pardus Event: Terminal ε

On September 16, 2010 — the same year the first JAMA paper was published, in the same institution — Paul Warren Pardus became distraught after learning his 84-year-old mother Jean Davis had been left paralyzed following spinal surgery intended to relieve compression and improve mobility. Receiving a post-operative update from Dr. David B. Cohen in the hallway, Pardus yelled "You ruined my mother," drew a handgun, and shot the surgeon in the abdomen. He then barricaded himself in his mother's room, fatally shot her, and took his own life. Dr. Cohen survived.

The Structural Reading

The Pardus case is not a tragedy of grief. It is a breakdown event after prolonged gradient misalignment with no correction channel. The violence is not the system — it's what happens when all feedback channels fail. The surgeon optimized for decompression success and mechanical stability. The patient and family were optimizing for the avoidance of irreversible decline, dignity, and continued mobility. The word "conservative" mapped to two entirely different variables in their respective loss functions. ε accumulated. The only channel left was the gun.

This is the clinical version of what the NeurIPS tutorial calls accumulated instability with no stepsize scheduler. In optimization, when no correction is applied across training steps, eventually the loss spikes beyond recovery. In surgery, when no loss function alignment is verified before the irreversible step, eventually the divergence finds a channel.

"You ruined my mother" is not irrationality. It is a gradient step taken by a system that had no other channel left. — Informed Consent Roundtable · Ukubona LLC · 2026

§4.1 The Father: Not Anecdote, But Bedrock

The informed-consent roundtable opens with a different case: a father, aged 67–70, who underwent three spinal surgeries across three years in Pretoria and Paris. His account of the consent process: "He didn't inform me about anything. Which one would you prefer? — You don't prefer without information."

This is not supporting evidence for the thesis. This is the ground truth from which the thesis was built. "Conservative" meant "ordinary operations" in his vocabulary — not the clinical meaning of watchful management. The consent process assumed a shared ontology that did not exist. The baseline parameters were corrupted before the first word of consent was spoken.

Layer V Bedrock

When a patient's calibration history (θt) is ignored at initialization, the entire downstream process — however technically correct — is operating on a false landscape. The gradient is real. The step is real. But the terrain it's descending is not the patient's terrain. This is not a communication failure. It is an ontological one.

Node 06 · Structural · Genomic Data Sharing

The Debbie DAG: Multi-Level Architecture

The genomic data sharing framework at ukubona-llc.github.io/debbie provides the multi-level structural picture that the clinical consent argument requires. It maps three theoretical zones — General Systems Theory, Principlism, and Distributive Justice — onto a directed acyclic graph spanning individual, institutional, national, and international levels.

DAG Structure (simplified)
GST → Awareness · Training · Attitudes · Practices [structural flow]
Principlism → Autonomy · Relational Factors · Community Engagement [ethical filter]
Distributive Justice → Power Dynamics · Equity · Outcome [equity lens]
Individual → Institutional → National → International

The Debbie framework was built for genomic data sharing. But it is, at its core, a formalization of the same problem: how do you align the values of individual participants (autonomy, benefit-sharing expectations, fear of re-identification) with institutional practices and national regulatory structures before a decision is made that affects them irreversibly?

§5.1 The Henrietta Lacks Node

The framework explicitly names Henrietta Lacks in the Relational Factors node. This is not decorative. Lacks represents the precise failure the entire DAG is designed to prevent: tissue taken without verified consent, used in ways the donor never imagined, benefiting institutions in ways that violated every weight she held. Her cells were a θ′ taken without Ltotal alignment. The update was irreversible. The ε is still accumulating.

From Genomics to Surgery

The Debbie DAG and the surgical consent framework are solving the same structural problem at different timescales and levels. Genomic data sharing is a slow-motion version of surgery: an irreversible extraction from the patient's body, processed through institutional and commercial machinery, generating outcomes the patient never authorized. The consent architecture required is identical. The loss function must be verified before the extraction, not disclosed afterward.

§5.2 LMIC vs HIC: The Disparity Is a Gradient

Distributive Justice in the Debbie framework focuses on the power asymmetry between Low- and Middle-Income Countries and High-Income Countries in genomic data sharing. Kituuka's emergency surgery data comes from Uganda — an LMIC hospital system operating under resource constraints that make even a basic consent conversation structurally difficult. The 2010 and 2014 JAMA papers are from the US. The K08 is a US NIH award. The disparity in the surgical risk data itself (Black donors at threefold higher perioperative mortality) connects all three.

This is not a coincidence. It is the same gradient at multiple scales: who gets to specify the weights, who benefits from the optimization, and who bears the ε.
