The Parable — Applied

"For I was hungry and you gave me food, I was thirsty and you gave me drink, I was a stranger and you welcomed me."

The patient in Kotido who never received a creatinine test is hungry for diagnosis. The district with no nephrologist is thirsty for capacity. The 72% of Ugandans invisible to the published literature are strangers the system has refused to welcome — not through malice, but through the architecture of neglect.

"I was naked and you clothed me, I was sick and you visited me, I was in prison and you came to me."

The blank district is the sick who were not visited. The missing record is the prisoner of end-stage renal failure who sold land to fund a trip to Kampala or Nairobi or New Delhi. The ε in the model — the error term, the residual, the thing traditional statistics discards — is the precise coordinate of this unvisited sickness.

"When did we see you hungry… or sick… or in prison, and visit you?" And the King will answer: "Truly I say to you, as you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me."

The question "when did we see you?" is the methodological crisis. You didn't see because you only measured the patients who reached the clinic. Ukubona — to see — demands a different architecture. One that asks not who came, but who never arrived, and why. The Atlas does not just report. It refuses to look away from the least of these.

The old method
Kubala
to count

Count the patients who reach the clinic. Measure who arrives. Build the dataset from the arrivals. The absent are invisible — their silence treated as informationless noise, or simply excluded from analysis.

Classical epidemiology. Clean. Tractable. Deeply unjust.

The new method
Kulaba
to see — truly

Contour the topology of who is missing. Map the gravity wells of absence. Ask not only who came, but who never got the chance. Treat the blank as signal. Let ε descend.

Matthew 25 epidemiology. Uncomfortable. Precise. Moral.

"Inasmuch as ye did it not to one of the least of these, ye did it not to me."
