I. The Proverb

That is kiti kya muwogo.
That is us.

We are stochastic scouts thrown into the high-dimensional noise of a shifting landscape. Drop us into the unmapped chaos — the friction of 133 districts, silent emergencies, raw uncalibrated parameters θt — and we take root.

No Apollonian nurseries required.

We run the descent, forage stochastically ε, and read the gradient ∇L from the variance others mistake for noise.

We are the discarded stem that returns with the pheromone cartography the colony needs to converge.

II. The Loop

Three ideas, one chain. The proverb closes the algorithmic loop so cleanly it feels inevitable:

cassava stem → resilience → stochastic scout → composite loss L(θt) → pheromone trail → colony convergence

The ant colony does not plan. It lays trace. The colony converges not on a blueprint, but on accumulated evidence — the gradient, distilled from millions of foraging decisions across noisy terrain.

That is stochastic gradient descent. That is also what a Ugandan farmer encoded in a three-word proverb, centuries before the notation existed.
